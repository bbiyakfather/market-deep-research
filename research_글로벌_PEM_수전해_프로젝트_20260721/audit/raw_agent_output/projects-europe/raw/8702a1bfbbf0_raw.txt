# Siemens Energy Delivers First Electrolysers to Air Liquide’s 200MW Hydrogen Plant in France

![Image 3: Fuel Cells Works](https://fuelcellsworks.com/_next/image?url=%2Fassets%2Fuser-icon-fcw.png&w=96&q=75)

By
Fuel Cells Works

September 24, 2025 at 10:19 AM EDT

![Image 4: FuelCellsWorks](https://s3.us-east-1.amazonaws.com/s3.ai.fuelcellsworks.com/siemens-energy-delivers-first-electrolysers-to-air-liquide-s-200mw-hydrogen-plant-in-france-2025-09-24T09%3A10%3A35-05%3A00-image.webp)

Images: Siemens-FCW

*   Siemens Energy has delivered the first nine of 12 PEM electrolysers to Air Liquide’s 200MW green hydrogen project in Port-Jérôme, France.
*   Backed by €400m ($470m) in investment, Normand’Hy will produce 28,000 tonnes of hydrogen annually starting in 2026—25% of which will fuel hydrogen trucks along the Seine Axis.

The first shipment of PEM electrolysers has arrived at Air Liquide’s flagship 200MW Normand’Hy project in Port-Jérôme, France, as construction progresses toward a 2026 commissioning date. Siemens Energy confirmed the delivery of nine out of twelve electrolysers built at its Berlin-based gigafactory—a joint venture with Air Liquide. The Berlin facility, one of the largest in Europe, currently boasts a 1GW annual stack production capacity, with plans to scale up to 3GW by 2025.

“With partnerships like these, we are not just talking about a greener future, we are shaping it,” Siemens Energy stated.

Normand’Hy, launched under the EU’s Important Projects of Common European Interest (IPCEI) programme, represents a €400m ($470m) investment by Air Liquide and is backed by the French government. Once operational, the plant will produce up to 28,000 tonnes of green hydrogen per year—expected to prevent 250,000 tonnes of CO₂ emissions annually, or the equivalent of 1.25 million round-trip flights between New York and London. Construction began in 2023, with full-scale production slated for next year.

About a quarter of the plant’s hydrogen output will serve transport refuelling stations along the Seine Axis, powering up to 500 hydrogen trucks. The remaining volumes are earmarked for industrial users including TotalEnergies. The project positions Port-Jérôme as a cornerstone in France’s national hydrogen strategy and marks a major milestone for Siemens Energy’s electrolysis deployment in Europe.
